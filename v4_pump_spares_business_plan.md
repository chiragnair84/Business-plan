# Business Plan: Niche Repeat-Demand Industrial Product

**Prepared:** October 2026  
**Business model:** Side business alongside employment; outsourced manufacturing and own-brand/white-label B2B distribution  
**Initial geography:** Anand → Vadodara → Ahmedabad → wider Gujarat  
**Target contribution:** 15–20%+ after manufacturing, packing and direct selling costs  
**Investment principle:** Validate demand first; start with one SKU and low inventory.

## 1. Executive summary

### Recommended starting hypothesis
Develop a specialist aftermarket business for **model-specific V4 submersible pump wear-part replacement kits**, initially investigating LTB-4 pump bushes, counter bushes and motor bushes.

Do not enter as a generic bush manufacturer. The proposed offer is **ready-to-fit replacement bush kits with clear model/fitment identification, consistent dimensions, reliable availability and small workshop packs**.

This is a hypothesis to validate, not a proven market gap. There are established manufacturers and sellers of these parts.

### Initial customers
- Pump repair workshops
- Submersible-pump dealers and spare-parts retailers
- Motor rewinding shops
- Agricultural pump service technicians
- Small pump manufacturers needing outsourced components

## 2. Why investigate this category?

- V4 submersible pumps are used in agriculture, irrigation and water-supply applications.
- Bushes/counter bushes are wear components found in multiple pump designs.
- The parts are small, courier-friendly and potentially suitable for outsourced CNC turning.
- Workshops may reorder when stock is consumed, creating repeat-demand potential.
- A specialist can differentiate through fitment knowledge, consistent quality, ready stock and quick fulfilment.

**Important:** Not all V4 pumps use identical parts. Do not finalize dimensions from online listings alone.

## 3. Niche and product strategy

Start with **one model-specific V4 bush or counter-bush SKU**. Only add SKUs after paid demand is proven.

Possible later expansion:
1. Additional V4 bush variants
2. Bush-and-sleeve kits
3. Other small, repeat-demand V4 wear parts
4. Selected V5/V6 or agricultural pump wear parts, only after validation

The defensible asset should be a **fitment database**, not just machining capability.

Record for every SKU:
- Brand, series and model/application
- Part name and reference sample
- OD, ID, length, tolerance and material
- Shaft size and relevant clearance
- Supplier, inspection method and revision
- Customers who confirmed fitment and any field feedback

## 4. Market validation before investment

### First 30-customer discovery exercise
Visit or call 30 pump repair businesses/dealers before placing a meaningful manufacturing order. Ask:

1. Which V4 pump brands/models do you service most?
2. Which bush/counter bush do you replace most often?
3. How many pieces or sets do you buy per month?
4. What price do you pay, and from whom?
5. What is inconvenient about the current supplier?
6. Would you test a consistent, ready-stock alternative?

Capture customer, pump brand/model, bush type, monthly quantity, current price, supplier and pain point.

### Selection criteria
Choose a first SKU only if:
- Multiple independent businesses report repeat consumption of the same application;
- At least five workshops/dealers agree to test samples;
- Fitment can be specified and inspected reliably;
- Three supplier quotations support the target margin; and
- Customers are willing to place paid orders, not merely express interest.

## 5. Manufacturing and quality

**Do not buy a CNC machine initially.** Outsource machining to established vendors and retain ownership of the specification, brand, inspection and customer relationship.

Build a vendor base with:
- Vendor A: CNC turning
- Vendor B: qualified backup
- Raw-material source
- Inspection/finishing support if required

Drawings should define material grade, OD, ID, length, tolerances, chamfers, surface finish where relevant and inspection method.

Quality controls:
- Inspect all dimensions on initial batches.
- Confirm material with the supplier and retain traceability.
- Conduct trial fitting with technicians before launch.
- Keep batch records and investigate every fitment complaint.
- Do not claim OEM equivalence without evidence or use OEM branding without permission.

## 6. Sales model for a founder with a full-time job

**Weekdays:** Phone/WhatsApp follow-up, quotations, order confirmation, vendor coordination and dispatch.  
**Saturdays:** Visit workshops and dealers, collect samples, map demand, demonstrate kits and obtain trial orders.  
**Holidays:** Longer territory visits and distributor development.

Start with direct B2B selling rather than depending on e-commerce or online leads alone. Use WhatsApp catalogue sheets with clear SKU, fitment, pack size, price and dispatch terms.

## 7. Pricing and working capital

Calculate the real landed cost:

**Landed cost = manufacturing + raw material (if separate) + finishing + inspection + packaging + freight + expected rejection/wastage.**

Then subtract direct selling and fulfilment costs when evaluating contribution. Do not assume the target margin is achievable until supplier quotes and customer prices are known.

- Initial inventory cap: **₹25,000–₹50,000** until repeat demand is proven.
- Start with one SKU, then two, then expand only from reorder evidence.
- First orders: advance/COD where possible.
- Offer short credit only to proven repeat buyers; avoid long credit cycles.
- Keep two qualified manufacturing sources where feasible.

## 8. 90-day execution plan

### Days 1–15: Discover
Interview 30 businesses. Record common brands, part types, monthly consumption, current prices and supply problems.

### Days 16–30: Select
Identify the most repeated application. Buy reference samples, measure them and obtain at least three quotations.

### Days 31–45: Prototype
Produce 20–50 samples through two vendors. Compare cost, dimensions, finish, consistency and delivery.

### Days 46–60: Field trial
Give samples to around 10 workshops and 5 dealers. Track fitment, performance, defects and willingness to pay.

### Days 61–90: Launch selectively
If results are positive, manufacture a small commercial batch, create labelled packaging, visit more customers and offer workshop packs.

## 9. Go / modify / stop criteria

**Proceed** if:
- At least 10 customers have tested the product;
- At least 5 customers reorder;
- Contribution is at least 15% after direct costs;
- Rejection/fitment issues are controlled;
- Two vendors can meet the specification; and
- Customers accept practical pack sizes.

**Modify** if demand exists but model variation, price or availability is the barrier.

**Stop** if repeat demand is weak, dimensions vary too much, quality cannot be controlled, customers require damaging credit terms or the viable contribution falls below the target.

## 10. Risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Incorrect fitment | Reference samples, controlled drawings and trial fitting |
| Material substitution | Approved material and vendor traceability |
| Strong price competition | Fitment support, ready stock, consistent quality and kits |
| Low repeat demand | Customer interviews and paid trials before inventory |
| Vendor dependency | Two qualified machining vendors |
| Too many SKUs | One-family-first strategy |
| Cash tied up in receivables | Strict credit limits |
| Product failure damages a pump | Appropriate specifications, inspection and batch traceability |

## 11. Competition and research limits

There are existing sellers/manufacturers of submersible-pump bushes and counter bushes, including Jagruti Industries, Vikas Submersible Spares and Parth Industries, among others. Therefore this is **not an empty market**.

The research supports that the category and parts exist and competition is established. It does **not** yet prove exact SKU-level monthly demand, search volume, a universal dimension or achievable margins. Those must be established in the field.

## 12. First action and final recommendation

Before spending meaningfully, complete the 30-customer discovery exercise and collect reference samples. Do not order a large batch based only on online research.

**Business concept:** Specialist aftermarket pump-spares supplier  
**First product hypothesis:** Model-specific V4 bush/counter-bush kits  
**Manufacturing:** Outsourced CNC turning  
**Sales:** Saturday field visits + weekday phone/WhatsApp  
**Inventory:** Low, one SKU first  
**Growth:** Prove repeat orders, then add adjacent parts for the same customers

The goal is not necessarily to find a product nobody makes. It is to find a part many customers repeatedly consume where the current buying experience is fragmented or inconvenient—and prove that with paid repeat orders before scaling.
